# Women in abstraction — verified claims (Mondrian Project)

**Kind:** DH technical research result report (claim table + source gate)  
**Status:** museum / published-source verified · **not** Ahmes-evaluator-safe coats  
**Scope:** claims that recur when students ask who invented the double line, who
preceded Kandinsky, or whether Pollock “got” drip from Sobel.  
**Product use:** `/about/` · seminar · historiography in
[`../corpus/REFERENCE-WORKS.md`](../corpus/REFERENCE-WORKS.md) — **not** MVP
catalog textures unless a rights row and D-12 armature clear.

## Method note

1. Each claim below is tagged **SUPPORTED**, **SUPPORTED with date variance**,
   or **UNSUPPORTED / overclaim**.
2. Primary load-bearing sources are museum pages and named essays with stable
   URLs or standard bibliographic identity. Secondary journalism is used only
   as corroboration.
3. Athanor scoped search on `mondrian-project` (2026-11-11) returned Mondrian /
   neoplasticism nodes, **not** Moss / af Klint / Sobel coats. Do not invent
   Tri-Anchors for these names until PDFs are procured and Ahmes-extracted.
4. Priority of abstraction ≠ contact history. Recovering a name ≠ changing
   institutional “greatness” criteria (Nochlin).

## Claim table

| # | Claim (classroom form) | Verdict | Evidence gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Hilma af Klint explored non-figurative painting before Kandinsky’s canonical abstract breakthroughs; that priority does **not** prove Kandinsky knew her work. | **SUPPORTED** | Af Klint Foundation: non-figurative works shown in her lifetime only in theosophical / anthroposophical contexts; international museum visibility from LACMA 1986 ([About](https://hilmaafklint.se/about-hilma-af-klint/), [Exhibitions](https://hilmaafklint.se/exhibitions/)). Hyperallergic / Tuchman interview: first major posthumous public survey context was *The Spiritual in Art* (LACMA, 23 Nov 1986 – 8 Mar 1987). |
| C2 | Marlow Moss introduced / tested the neoplastic **double line** around **1930**; Mondrian began using parallel double lines around **1932**, after asking Moss to explain the device. | **SUPPORTED with date variance** | [Kunstsammlung NRW — Mondrian ch. 5 / Marlow Moss](https://www.kunstsammlung.de/en/mondrian/chapter05): Moss tested the double line in **1930**, “two years before Mondrian begins using” it; Mondrian later used parallel lines without crediting Moss. Tate Howarth essay: Moss’s double-line paintings brought her to Mondrian’s attention **1931**; device later adopted by Mondrian, Gorin, others ([Tate essay](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artists/marlow-moss-1671/essay)). Stedelijk Studies: double lines visible in **1931** works published in *Abstraction-Création* (1932). **Do not collapse 1930 / 1931 into one reel hook** — record both museum page and essay dates. |
| C3 | Moss’s rationale for the double line was dynamism / interrelated rhythm in space (vs static single-line plane splits). | **SUPPORTED** (quoted via Nijhoff / letters) | Kunstsammlung NRW quotes Moss’s three-point explanation ending: the double line makes “a continuity of related and inter-related rhythm in space” possible. Tate Howarth cites Nijhoff quoting Moss: purpose was to render “the composition dynamic instead of static.” |
| C4 | Mondrian did not publicly assign authorship of the double line to Moss; correspondence shows incomplete theoretical agreement. | **SUPPORTED** | Kunstsammlung NRW: Mondrian admits to Jean Gorin he did not understand Moss’s theory, yet used parallel lines. Art Newspaper (2026 show coverage): Vantongerloo urged credit; Mondrian–Moss coolness until ~1936 (“I am back to usual with her”). |
| C5 | Much of Moss’s pre-war oeuvre was destroyed in WWII (Normandy / Gauciel store). | **SUPPORTED** | Tate Howarth: “much of her output from before 1939 was destroyed during the war.” Haus Konstruktiv: 1944 bombardment destroyed works stored in Gauciel. Cornwall Artists Index / Guardian 2014: Château d’Évreux (Gauciel) laid waste; most early work lost. |
| C6 | Tate reserved two Moss works in **1973** and the Trustees **declined** the purchase; a pre-war work entered later. | **SUPPORTED** | Tate Howarth: Norman Reid reserved two works at Gimpel & Hanover, Zürich (1973); Trustees decided against; Ronald Alley “rather sad.” Later acquisition: *White and Yellow* (1935). Archive letters cited in the Tate publication. |
| C7 | Clement Greenberg’s **1961** *Art and Culture* revision of “‘American-Type’ Painting” states that Pollock (and Greenberg) saw Janet Sobel at Peggy Guggenheim’s in **1944** and that Pollock later admitted the pictures made an impression — while calling Sobel a “primitive” painter and “housewife.” | **SUPPORTED** | Greenberg, *Art and Culture* (Beacon, 1961), “‘American-Type’ Painting” (~p. 218 in common reprints / Monoskop scan). Sandra Zalman, *Woman’s Art Journal* 36.2 (2015): documents the 1955→1961 edit and the domesticating labels. MoMA holds *Milky Way* (1945) as a key Sobel drip/all-over. |
| C8 | Greenberg’s testimony proves Sobel “invented” Pollock’s drip / that Pollock copied her. | **UNSUPPORTED / overclaim** | Same Greenberg paragraph immediately re-asserts Pollock’s mural (early 1944) and 1946 enamel “boldness and breadth unparalleled by anything seen in Sobel or Tobey.” Correct classroom form: **documented impression + all-over precedence in Greenberg’s own looking**, not sole causal authorship. |
| C9 | Linda Nochlin’s “Why Have There Been No Great Women Artists?” (1971) argues institutional obstacles, not innate incapacity; recovering names is necessary but not sufficient. | **SUPPORTED** | First widely cited form: *ARTnews*, January 1971; also *Woman in Sexist Society* (1971) under a related title; collected in *Women, Art, and Power* (1988). Full text republished by ARTnews (2015). |

## Distinctions the product must keep

1. **Priority ≠ contact** (C1). Af Klint’s earlier non-figurative practice does
   not rewrite Kandinsky’s reception history by itself.
2. **Moss ≠ “Mondrian’s whole language”** (C2–C4). The precise neoplastic claim
   for this repo is the **double line** (and related truncated / relief
   experiments), not wholesale invention of neoplasticism.
3. **Stories are not identical** (C1, C5–C8). Limited theosophical circulation
   → 1986 visibility (af Klint); wartime loss + institutional refusal (Moss);
   Guggenheim exposure + domesticating critical label (Sobel).
4. **Nochlin over recovery alone** (C9). Adding Moss to an About paragraph
   without changing selection / measurement / rights practice is incomplete.
